## When to use

- For a trend: monthly consumption, intensity per year, a grid factor over time.
- To compare two or three series that share a unit.

## When not to use

- For comparing a few categories at one moment. Use a bar chart.
- For parts of a total. Use a stacked bar chart.
- For a single latest figure. Use a Stat.

## Anatomy

Grid and axes, one line per series with a marker per point (every point when there are 24 or fewer, otherwise only the active one), a guide line and halo on the active category, and a tooltip.

## Colour and shape

Series take `dataviz.cat-1` to `cat-8` in order and a marker shape (circle, square, diamond, triangle). The legend and tooltip repeat the shape.

## Notes

- `variant` is `line` (default) or `step`.
- Categories are evenly spaced. For unevenly spaced time, resample to a regular axis first.
- `null` breaks the line; the tooltip and data table say “No data”.
- `references` draws target (heavy dash) and reference (dotted) lines across the plot, labelled with their value. They are also rows at the foot of the data table.
- `toggleSeries` lets people hide series from the legend; control it with `hiddenSeries` and `onHiddenSeriesChange`, or start from `defaultHiddenSeries`. At least one series always stays visible, and series keep their colour and marker shape.
- Smoothing is not offered: a curve implies values that were not measured.

## Screen readers

- A figure named by its title with a data table fallback. Arrow keys announce the category and every series' value.

- With `toggleSeries`, each legend entry is a button with `aria-pressed` (true = shown). Hiding or showing is announced politely, for example “Market-based hidden”. The last visible series is marked `aria-disabled` and stays on.
- Hidden series leave the drawing, the tooltip, the live announcements and the data table, so what is read matches what is seen.
- Target and reference lines are listed at the foot of the data table with their kind, label and value, for example “Target: 2030 pathway: 14,000 tCO₂e”.

## Visual

- Lines are two pixels thick against the surface, and marker shapes differ per series so colour is never the only cue.
- On hover, the nearest category is chosen, so thin lines do not need precise aim.
