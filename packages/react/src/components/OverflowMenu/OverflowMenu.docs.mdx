## When to use

- In table rows, cards and list items that have several secondary actions.
- In a toolbar that runs out of room.

## When not to use

- For the one action most people take. Give it its own [Button](/components/button) or [Icon button](/components/icon-button).
- When the trigger should say what the menu is for. Use a [Menu button](/components/menu-button).
- For more than about seven items or for navigation. Use a different pattern.

## Writing

Put the most common action first, a separator before destructive items, and the destructive item last, marked `intent="danger"`. Destructive items open a confirmation.

## Anatomy

An Icon button with a three-dot icon (the label doubles as its tooltip) and a Menu panel. The panel aligns to the end of the trigger so it opens inward from the edge of a row.

## Notes

- `label` is required and becomes the accessible name. Include the object's name when several menus share a page.
- Children are the regular menu parts (`DropdownMenuItem`, `DropdownMenuSeparator`, groups, checkbox and radio items).
- Controlled use: `open` and `onOpenChange`.

## Keyboard

<Keys rows={[["Enter, Space or ArrowDown", "Opens the menu and focuses the first item."], ["ArrowDown, ArrowUp", "Moves between items."], ["Type a letter", "Jumps to a matching item."], ["Escape", "Closes the menu and returns focus to the button."]]} />

## Screen readers

- The button is announced with its label and as a menu button (`aria-haspopup="menu"`), expanded or collapsed.

## Visual

- The target is at least the button size. Focus shows the standard ring.
