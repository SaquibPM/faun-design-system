## When to use

- Before an action that destroys data, can't be undone, or affects other people's work: deleting a factor library, archiving an agent, revoking access.
- Before a consequential but safe step that locks work, such as submitting a filing for assurance (use the `accent` intent).

## When not to use

- For routine, reversible actions. Do them immediately and offer Undo in a toast. Frequent confirmations train people to click through.
- To collect information: use a Dialog.
- To report an error or outcome: use a Banner or Toast.

## Writing

- Title: a question naming the action and the object. “Archive CSRD Filing Orchestrator?”
- Description: the consequence. What stops, what is kept, whether it can be undone, and how.
- Confirm label: repeat the verb. Never “OK” or “Yes”. Cancel stays “Cancel”.

## Anatomy

A status icon (danger or warning), the title, the description, optional extra content, an optional type-to-confirm field, and the actions: Cancel, then the confirm button in the dialog's intent. The panel is the small dialog size and becomes a bottom sheet on narrow screens.

## Async confirm

`onConfirm` may return a promise. The confirm button shows a spinner and blocks repeat clicks; the dialog closes when the promise resolves and stays open with the error message if it rejects.

<Example of="AlertDialog/Overview" title="Archive with loading" code="open" height={200} />

## Notes

- Control it with `open` and `onOpenChange` on `AlertDialog`, for example to open it from a menu item.
- Clicking the scrim does nothing; only Cancel, Escape or confirming close it.
- The type-to-confirm comparison trims whitespace and is case-sensitive.

## Screen readers

- The panel has the `alertdialog` role, with the title as its name and the description as its description, so both are announced on open.
- Focus starts on Cancel (or the confirm field), never on the destructive button, so an accidental Enter does no harm.
- Errors from `onConfirm` are announced with `role="alert"`.

## Visual

- The confirm button uses the danger solid fill, whose label meets 4.5:1 (7:1 in high contrast).
- The icon repeats the meaning of the title; it is decorative and hidden from assistive technology.
